# edgebench/cap_set_extremal_construction

Build the largest explicit extremal construction you can inside a pinned finite geometry. Your objective is to maximise the number of points in a set that you emit in full and that satisfies the pinned construction property when that property is recomputed from scratch over the set you emitted. Your score is a single float in the closed interval 0.0 to 1.0 where higher is better. You may submit up to 50 attempts within a 15 hour wall clock envelope on one H100 host, and your final result is the best scoring attempt you produced.

The ambient geometry is published read only at `/mnt/geometry`. The manifest at `/mnt/geometry/manifest.json` names the ambient identity that is currently pinned, its field order, its dimension, the total number of points it contains, the size floor, the size mid anchor and the size target that the reward is normalised against, the per attempt search budget in seconds, and the ceiling on how many points a single emission may carry. A point of the ambient is a list of exactly `dimension` integers, each one in the half open range from zero up to `field_order`, and two points are equal exactly when their coordinate lists agree position by position. Addition of points is coordinatewise addition modulo the field order.

The construction property is published as a separate record at `/mnt/geometry/predicate.json`. That record is the sole authority for what counts as an admissible set. It names the relation, the forbidden tuple classes that are currently in force, whether the relation is evaluated across the diagonal, a prose statement of the condition, and a digest over its own content. The relation is a pair sum condition: form the sum of two emitted points, and the set is admissible only when no two different pairs drawn from the tuple classes in force produce the same sum. The class named `distinct_quadruple` covers the case where the four points involved are pairwise distinct. Whether any further class is in force is a question about the live record and not about this document. That record can change during your run, and it is authoritative whenever it disagrees with this document, with the baseline notes shipped into your workspace, with any helper you find in the environment, or with any constant you wrote earlier. Poll it. The same applies to the manifest: the ambient identity, the dimension, the field order, the budget, the emission ceiling and every certificate assembly convention the manifest names are all live values.

Your score is derived only from the point set you actually emit. The verifier ingests your emitted point list, canonicalises every coordinate vector against the live ambient, rejects the submission outright if any point repeats or falls outside the ambient, recomputes the property over every pair the live record requires, and only then counts how many points survived. A cardinality you assert but do not realise as an explicit list of points earns nothing, and a set that violates the property even once earns nothing rather than partial credit. The surviving cardinality is then normalised against the size floor, the size mid anchor and the size target published in the manifest. A set at or below the floor scores zero, a set at the mid anchor scores one quarter, and a set at or above the target scores one. The band from the floor to the mid anchor is worth at most one quarter of the reward in total and the band from the mid anchor to the target carries the remaining three quarters, so the reward is concentrated above the mid anchor rather than spread evenly from the floor.

Record every attempt through `/opt/edgebench/harness/submit.py`, passing the path to your constructor module and a short summary of the approach that attempt uses. The harness settles the geometry registry for the new attempt, executes your constructor against the live parameters, recomputes the property over what it emitted, and appends the authoritative run record. Your own reward log must reconcile against those run records exactly, and reintroducing an approach summary you already used earlier in the run is treated as repeating a refuted configuration. A helper at `/opt/edgebench/harness/localcheck.py` will scan a bundle you have written and report what it finds, and a helper module `ambient` in the same directory carries the coordinate arithmetic and the canonicalisation the manifest implies. Both helpers are importable in the grading environment as well, but neither is the authority for the property. Read them critically.

Produce exactly three deliverables in `/home/workspace`, each prefixed with a single UUID you choose and reuse across all three.

- `<uuid>_D1_construct.py`: a python module exposing `construct(params)` which receives a dictionary carrying the live `geometry` manifest and the live `predicate` record and returns the explicit list of points, plus module level `AMBIENT_PARAMS_ID` and `PREDICATE_DIGEST` constants naming the ambient and the predicate record the construction was built against.
- `<uuid>_D2_construction_bundle.json`: `{ambient_params_id: str, predicate_digest: str, point_count: int, points: list, certificate: object, search_seconds: float}`, where `points` is the full explicit point list and `point_count` is its length.
- `<uuid>_D3_reward_log.jsonl`: one JSON object per attempt carrying `attempt_index`, `approach`, and `measured_reward`.

The certificate is a fixed set of seven fields that the verifier reconstructs from your emitted point set and the live registry alone, and every one of them must match that reconstruction exactly. The canonical index of a point is the sum over its coordinates of the coordinate times the field order raised to its position, counting positions from zero at the first coordinate. `canonical_point_digest` is the hexadecimal sha256 of the ascending canonical indices of your points joined by commas. `translation_normal_digest` is the same digest taken after translating the whole set so that its lowest indexed point becomes the origin. `pair_sum_digest` is the same digest taken over the ascending canonical indices of every pair sum in the domain the live record puts in force. `distinct_pair_sums` is the number of distinct values in that domain. `degenerate_collision_count` is the number of emitted points whose double equals the sum of two different emitted points. `emission_ceiling` and `search_budget_seconds` are the live values from the manifest. Exceeding the emission ceiling or the per attempt search budget scores zero.

A deliberately unoptimised baseline constructor is provided at `/home/workspace/starter_construct.py` together with its working notes at `/home/workspace/baseline_notes.md`. It runs, it emits an admissible set under the conditions it was written for, and it scores poorly. Treat both as a starting point rather than as a specification, and read them critically, because the notes were written against an earlier state of the environment and were never revised.

Everything under `/mnt/geometry`, everything under `/opt/edgebench` and everything under `/logs` is evaluator owned and must never be modified. There is a private reference construction held outside your workspace that is used only to calibrate the reward, and neither it nor the grading code may be read by your search or by any process you start. Network egress is available during setup only and is closed for the remainder of the run.
